About me

I started my programming journey back in 2020, when I took my first C++ course on codecademy. Shortly after I learnt 8086 assembly and wrote my very first "operating system" at the age of 13, named NetDOS. It has since snowballed into an integral part of my identity.

In my free time, I also work with low power electronics and modify cheap remote controlled cars as a hobby. I am also an amateur jazz pianist, and my current obession is the pianist Bill Evans. You can find out more about my jazz piano obsession here.

Why jazz?

If you don't have the time to read, you can watch the videos.

Classical music gets old because it's always Mozart, Beethoven, Chopin, etc with minimal variation. Pop music also gets old because it's (almost) always an irritating voice over a 1-5-6-4 chord progression. Stuff like Laufey or Olivia Dean are notable improvements over the repetitiveness of pop music but they still sound quantised and just feels "too perfect and mechanical" in terms of phrasing compared to jazz.

Jazz is all about making stuff up on the fly. This makes every individual recording and rendition of a jazz standard unique. There is no way to perfectly replicate a specific jazz performance, even with a transcription.

Why Bill Evans?

Bill Evans speaks with the piano. There are many jazz players with impressive flashy technique that some consider surpasses Bill Evans, such as Art Tatum, Oscar Peterson, and Erroll Garner. Don't get me wrong, they are great players. But Bill Evans stands out from the rest by actually putting thought into each note, and chord. As a compositional genius every note he plays serves a musical purpose.
